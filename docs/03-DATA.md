## 5. Pack files (schema; P1 builds them)

```
data/packs/meridian-arc/
  company.yaml                 identity, units, FY convention, segments, speakers, analysts
  financials_quarterly.csv     one row per quarter: revenue ... pat, ocf, capex, net_debt, wc days
  segments_quarterly.csv       quarter, segment, revenue, ebit
  guidance.yaml                G*, S*, D* above: id, date, speaker, verbatim, metric, target, period, transcript ref
  consensus.yaml               street estimates for the current quarter
  notes/Q2FY27-notes.md        selected notes to accounts (discom receivables, capex, borrowings)
  commentary/Q2FY27-prepared-remarks-draft.md   the draft the team wants checked
  transcripts/Q4FY26-call.md   speaker-tagged, every line has a stable id like Q4FY26-L042
  transcripts/Q1FY27-call.md
```

Transcripts are about 3,000 to 4,000 words each, with three fictional analyst firms. Fact-bearing text (transcripts, notes, remarks) is written by a strong model and checked by `scripts/validate_pack.py`, which recomputes every figure in section 3, checks the reconciliations, and confirms each verbatim statement in section 4 appears at its transcript ref.

## 6. Assumptions (stated in the README and in the pitch)

1. The company and every figure are synthetic, built to reproduce the situation in the brief.
2. Consensus estimates stand in for a street consensus a real IR team would buy.
3. Investor salience weights (`04-SCORING.md` section 1) are judgement calls from typical earnings-call practice, not fitted to data.
4. Scores rank answer quality against a rubric. They do not predict share-price reaction.
5. Disclosure flags are prompts for review by the company secretary, not legal advice.
6. Only the shipped pack is supported in the prototype; the schema is designed so a real company's pack could be dropped in later.
